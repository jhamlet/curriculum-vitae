# Strategic/Executive Hiring Practices Research

**Research Date:** 2026-01-05 **Subject:** How VPs of Engineering and
CTOs at growth-stage companies (100-500 employees) evaluate
strategic/senior hires **Sources:** Andreessen Horowitz, Elad Gil High
Growth Handbook, executive recruiting research, VP/CTO hiring blogs,
LeadDev

---

## Key Findings

1. **Executive hiring takes 130 days on average.** A CEO can only
   effectively run 2 executive searches simultaneously. This is one of
   the most time-intensive decisions a leader makes.

2. **Builders vs. operators distinction is critical.** Growth-stage
   companies need executives who have _built_ organizations, not just
   operated within them. Working at a large company managing existing
   programs doesn't translate to building programs from scratch.

3. **The 12-18 month rule governs hiring scope.** Hire for someone who
   can scale the function for the next 12-18 months. Anything shorter
   and they won't add enough value relative to hiring time; too senior
   and they'll get bored.

4. **Strategic thinking is assessed through business impact, not
   credentials.** Executives want to see technology decisions connected
   to revenue, market expansion, or organizational transformation - not
   technology for technology's sake.

5. **The 100-day plan test is common for finalists.** Having candidates
   present their first 100 days to the executive team reveals strategic
   thinking, assignment response, and communication quality.

6. **"IC who leads" requires demonstrating influence without
   authority.** Staff+ engineers are evaluated on cross-team impact,
   architectural leadership, and organizational influence - not just
   technical output.

## Executive Evaluation Process

### How VPs/CTOs Approach Strategic Hiring

#### Time Investment Reality

| Activity                  | Executive Time | Typical Frequency   |
| ------------------------- | -------------- | ------------------- |
| Executive search          | 130 days avg   | Concurrent limit: 2 |
| Reference checking        | 30-60 min      | Per finalist        |
| 100-day plan presentation | 2-3 hours      | 2 finalists         |
| Daily check-ins (onboard) | 30 min/day     | First 2 weeks       |

Source:
[a16z Executive Hiring](https://a16z.com/the-first-principles-of-executive-hiring/)

#### The CEO's Role

Ali Ghodsi (Databricks) on executive hiring: "Spend the energy, spend
the time. This is one of the most important decisions you could ever
make as a leader in your company."

For unfamiliar functions, CEOs should: - Meet 3-4 great people in that
role at companies a few years ahead - Use investors/mentors to identify
excellence benchmarks - Standardize hiring process beyond what feels
natural - Have senior ICs evaluate technical leaders when appropriate

Source:
[High Growth Handbook - Hiring Executives](https://growth.eladgil.com/book/chapter-4-building-the-executive-team/hiring-executives/)

### Resume Screening Process (30 Seconds to 2 Minutes)

**What gets noticed first:**

1. Most recent experience and its relevance
2. Career progression and increasing responsibility
3. Quantifiable impact and business outcomes
4. Keywords matching role requirements
5. Professional summary (if compelling)

**Reality:** Recruiters spend 5-30 seconds on initial review. Senior
roles get more attention (30 seconds to 2 minutes) but still require
front-loaded key information.

Source:
[Standout CV Research](https://standout-cv.com/usa/stats-usa/how-long-recruiters-spend-looking-at-resume/)

## Strategic Thinking Signals

### What Indicates Org-Level Thinking on a Resume

#### Business Impact Framing

**Strong signals:**

- Technology decisions connected to revenue growth or market expansion
- Digital transformation leading to measurable business outcomes
- Strategic initiatives aligned with company objectives
- Cost optimization with specific figures
- Technical roadmaps tied to business goals

**Weak signals:**

- Technology implementations without business context
- Feature delivery without outcome measurement
- Process improvements without scale or impact metrics

Source:
[Louvion VP/CTO Resume Guide](https://www.louvion.com/blog/mastering-the-art-of-the-resume-the-vp-engineering-and-cto-guide)

#### Time Horizon Thinking

CTOs maintain 4-6 year roadmaps (68% per Gartner 2025 survey). VPs of
Engineering operate in quarterly OKR cycles. Resumes should reflect
appropriate horizon:

- Staff/Principal: Quarterly to annual technical initiatives
- Director: Annual to multi-year program delivery
- VP/CTO: Multi-year strategic planning and execution

Source:
[DigitalDefynd CTO vs VP Engineering](https://digitaldefynd.com/IQ/cto-vs-vp-engineering/)

#### Evidence of Strategic Leadership

**What executives look for:**

- "Developed and executed technical roadmaps aligned with business
  objectives"
- "Collaborated with executive leadership to define engineering best
  practices"
- "Spearheaded digital transformation resulting in X% revenue impact"
- "Led strategic planning for 5-year technology vision"

**What they dismiss:**

- Generic descriptions of daily tasks
- Technology lists without application context
- Vague "contributed to" language
- Process compliance without outcomes

Source:
[Resume Worded Senior Director Examples](https://resumeworded.com/senior-director-of-engineering-resume-example)

## Cross-Functional Evidence

### How Executives Assess Breadth of Impact

#### Language That Signals Cross-Functional Capability

**High-value terms:**

- "Cross-functional" / "Interdepartmental" / "Multidisciplinary"
- "Stakeholder alignment across organization levels"
- "Bridge between technical staff and non-technical stakeholders"
- "Matrixed structures" navigation

**Resume example:** "Led cross-functional team of 18 engineers to
redesign microservices architecture, reducing system latency by 42% and
increasing deployment frequency from bi-weekly to daily while
maintaining 99.99% uptime."

Source:
[Teal Cross-Functional Resume Guide](https://www.tealhq.com/resume-synonyms/cross-functional)

#### Organizational Influence Indicators

Research from IEEE shows engineers with demonstrated leadership skills
in IC roles show:

- 34% higher team alignment scores
- 41% greater cross-functional influence during initial leadership
  tenure

This creates "pre-positional leadership credibility" - leadership
capability recognized before formal authority.

Source:
[ResearchGate - Engineer to Leader Transition](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391544658_From_Engineer_to_Leader_Navigating_the_Technical_Leadership_Transition)

#### Specific Metrics That Demonstrate Breadth

- Number of teams/departments coordinated
- Budget responsibility across functions
- Stakeholder levels engaged (IC to C-suite)
- Project scope crossing organizational boundaries
- Standards/practices adopted org-wide

## Communication Assessment

### What "Executive-Ready" Communication Looks Like

#### Resume as Communication Sample

The resume itself demonstrates communication skills. Executives
evaluate:

- **Conciseness:** Summaries under a paragraph, bullets under 6 per role
- **Clarity:** Complex concepts explained simply
- **Specificity:** Concrete numbers over vague claims
- **Relevance:** Tailored to role, not generic

**Strong example:** "Presented quarterly financial updates to the board,
securing approval for a $10 million capital investment."

**Weak example:** "Excellent communication skills" (unsupported claim)

Source:
[REN Network Resume Writing for Senior Leaders](https://ren-network.com/resume-writing-101-for-senior-leaders/)

#### Technical-to-Business Translation

VPs of Engineering must bridge technical and non-technical stakeholders.
Resumes should demonstrate:

- Translating technical concepts for executives/customers
- Connecting engineering decisions to business outcomes
- Communicating complex trade-offs clearly
- Sales/customer-facing communication (especially important for
  sales-led companies)

a16z specifically notes: "They should be able to explain deeply
technical concepts to nontechnical peers and customers."

Source:
[a16z Hiring SVP Engineering](https://a16z.com/hiring-a-senior-vice-president-of-engineering/)

#### Formatting Best Practices

- Clean, professional design (ATS-compatible)
- 10-12 point fonts (Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman)
- Consistent structure
- No elaborate templates or excessive graphics
- Bullet points focused on achievements, not duties

Source:
[Resume Worded Senior Engineering Manager](https://resumeworded.com/senior-engineering-manager-resume-example)

## Contract-to-Hire for Strategic Roles

### Special Considerations for This Engagement Type

#### Why Contract-to-Hire is Popular for Senior Roles

- Reduces risk of costly bad hires at senior levels
- Allows evaluation of cultural fit over time
- Provides observation of decision-making and strategic thinking
- Enables assessment of team dynamics and collaboration
- Typical duration: 3-6 months (senior), up to 12 months (specialized)

Source:
[AIHR Contract-to-Hire Guide](https://www.aihr.com/hr-glossary/contract-to-hire/)

#### Challenges for Strategic Roles Specifically

**Critical consideration:** Strategy- and leadership-based roles may not
get the same results in contract positions versus permanent roles.

**Why:**

- Strategic thinking requires long-term investment mindset
- Authority and trust build over time
- Innovation requires psychological safety
- Leadership effectiveness depends on team commitment

**Mitigation:** Clearly define strategic expectations in scope of work.
Set explicit evaluation criteria for leadership behaviors, not just
deliverables.

Source:
[AccruePartners Role Transition Guide](https://www.accruepartners.com/blogs/how-to-transition-full-time-roles-to-contract-roles)

#### Evaluation Criteria During Trial Period

**Beyond technical delivery:**

- Cultural fit and values alignment
- Leadership style demonstration
- Proactive problem identification
- Strategic initiative and foresight
- Communication with stakeholders
- Team integration and collaboration

**Conversion indicators:**

- Takes lead in projects to showcase leadership skills
- Goes beyond call of duty
- Proactively identifies risks and implements mitigation
- Demonstrates foresight and strategic thinking

Source:
[FasterCapital Contract-to-Hire Transition](https://fastercapital.com/content/Contract-to-hire--Transitioning-from-Contract-to-Hire-to-Full-Time-Employment.html)

## Delegation Patterns

### What Executives Look At vs What They Pass to HMs

#### Standard Screening Flow

1. **ATS/HR Screen:** Keyword matching, basic qualifications (50 → 15
   candidates)
2. **Recruiter Review:** Role fit, communication quality, red flags
3. **Hiring Manager Review:** Technical depth, culture signals,
   progression
4. **Executive Review:** Strategic fit, leadership potential, business
   impact

For senior/strategic roles, executives often engage earlier in the
process.

Source:
[Indeed How Hiring Managers Review Resumes](https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/interviewing/reviewing-resumes)

#### What CTOs Cannot Delegate

CTOs can delegate:

- Organizational architecture (to VP Engineering)
- Technical strategy details (to Staff/Principal engineers)
- Day-to-day execution (to Directors/Managers)

CTOs must handle personally:

- Contributing to overall business strategy as executive team member
- Final accountability for technology strategy
- Strategic alignment with business objectives

Source:
[Make Me a CTO Newsletter](https://makemeacto.substack.com/p/the-often-overlooked-truth-about-ctos-responsibilities-8a76359c9ab4)

#### What Executives Review Personally for Senior Hires

**Direct executive attention for:**

- Strategic thinking evidence
- Business impact narrative
- Leadership and influence signals
- Cultural and values alignment
- Career trajectory and growth pattern

**Delegated to hiring managers/ICs:**

- Technical skill verification
- Detailed experience validation
- Team fit assessment
- Day-to-day capability evaluation

a16z note: "In some cases, an individual contributor (IC), rather than
another executive, may be the best person to evaluate a particular skill
set. This is often the case when hiring technical leaders, such as an
SVP of engineering, where a senior IC engineer may be most able to
assess their technical abilities."

Source: [a16z Executive Hiring](https://a16z.com/executive-hiring/)

## IC Who Leads vs Leader Who Codes

### Distinguishing Senior Technical Leadership Types

#### Staff/Principal Engineer (IC Who Leads)

**Defining characteristics:**

- Technical depth: Authority on specific systems, datastores, runtimes
- Influence through expertise, not authority
- Cross-team problem unblocking
- Architecture and design direction
- Mentoring and technical guidance

**How they create impact:**

- "Staff+ engineers review the most complex design docs, unblock tricky
  cross-team problems, mentor seniors, and write things that shape how
  the org builds software."
- Impact measured by clarity brought, technical direction set, and
  organizational influence wielded

Source:
[LeadDev - Staff/Principal/Distinguished Engineers](https://leaddev.com/career-development/who-are-staff-principal-and-distinguished-engineers)

#### Engineering Manager/Director (Leader Who Codes)

**Defining characteristics:**

- People management responsibility
- Team delivery accountability
- Hiring, performance, career development
- Process and project management
- May maintain some coding involvement

**How they create impact:**

- Team outcomes and delivery
- Organizational scaling
- Talent development and retention
- Process optimization

#### Resume Differentiation

**For IC Who Leads:**

- Highlight architectural leadership upfront
- Show cross-team influence
- Demonstrate "influence without authority"
- Quantify technical impact (performance, reliability, adoption)
- Show breadth of organizational influence

**For Leader Who Codes:**

- Lead with team size and growth
- Show delivery outcomes
- Highlight people development
- Balance technical credibility with leadership outcomes

Source:
[Teal Staff Software Engineer Resume](https://www.tealhq.com/resume-example/staff-software-engineer)

#### Influence Without Authority

Key skill for senior ICs. Demonstrated through:

- Persuading teams to converge on shared standards
- Technical trade-off alignment with business risk
- Budget negotiation
- External partner relationships

Rule of thumb: "If you can't influence a team in the direction you think
is best, then either your influence needs work or the team knows better
than you."

Source:
[Medium - What Staff Engineers Actually Do](https://medium.com/staff-thinking/what-staff-engineers-actually-do-and-why-its-not-just-code-b535254e8eaa)

## Builder vs Operator Assessment

### Critical Distinction for Growth-Stage Hiring

#### The Builder Archetype

**Defining traits:**

- Has created programs, not just run them
- Built organizations, not just worked in them
- Passionate about building teams and companies
- Views unanswered questions as opportunities
- Fanatical about finding and coaching talent

**As Ben Horowitz puts it:** "The person who works at a company isn't
the person who built the company. The person who works in an
organization is not the one who built it. They really do need to have
built it."

Source:
[a16z Hard Things About Scaling](https://a16z.com/the-hard-things-about-scaling-executive-hiring/)

#### The Operator Archetype

**Defining traits:**

- Expert at managing existing programs
- Skilled at optimization and efficiency
- Strong at process and execution
- May struggle with ambiguity
- Excels in structured environments

**Common failure mode:** Hiring a great operator for a builder role. "An
operator is going to step on the gas and you may find yourself going
sideways in a hurry."

Source:
[Medium - Founders, Builders, Operators](https://medium.com/@rdli/founders-builders-and-operators-8a76359c9ab4)

#### How to Signal "Builder" on Resume

**Evidence of building:**

- "Established" / "Created from scratch" / "Built"
- Zero-to-one initiatives
- New team/function formation
- Process creation (not just execution)
- Scaling from small to large

**Evidence of operating (weaker for growth-stage):**

- "Managed" / "Oversaw" / "Maintained"
- Optimization of existing systems
- Process compliance and execution
- Large-team management without growth narrative

## Onboarding Expectations

### How Executives Evaluate New Hires Post-Start

#### The First Two Weeks Test

Ben Horowitz recommends for new executives:

"For the first two weeks every day at six o'clock, you and I are going
to sit down and I want to know everything you did today."

Purpose: Early detection of whether new hire is applying old playbook vs
learning new context.

Warning: "Outside executives come into your company and they do the job
that they did at their last company because that's what they know how to
do. That is almost always a big mistake."

Source:
[a16z Executive Hiring](https://a16z.com/the-first-principles-of-executive-hiring/)

#### The 100-Day Plan

Final-stage evaluation often includes presenting a 100-day plan to
executive team:

**What it reveals:**

- Assignment response quality
- Strategic thinking capability
- Communication with senior stakeholders
- Balance of listening vs acting
- Understanding of company context

**Structure executives expect:**

- Days 1-30: Listen, learn, assess current state
- Days 31-60: Develop strategy, identify priorities
- Days 61-100: Execute initial wins, establish foundation

## Warning Signs Executives Watch For

### Red Flags in Strategic Hiring

#### Resume Red Flags

- Vague descriptions without specific contributions
- Technology lists without business context
- Job hopping without clear narrative
- "Contributed to" without ownership claims
- Over-promising credentials

#### Interview Red Flags

- Can't explain work simply
- Takes credit for team outcomes inappropriately
- Doesn't ask questions about company/role
- Speaks negatively about previous employers
- Can't say "I don't know"

#### Leadership Red Flags

When existing leaders show these signs, they've been outgrown:

- "When you're asking other people, 'What do you think?' Or, when you're
  starting to have thoughts like, 'Is this the right person or is it not
  the right person?' You're done."
- "If you give somebody a task and they get super emotional, they're
  done."

Source:
[High Growth Handbook](https://growth.eladgil.com/book/chapter-4-building-the-executive-team/hiring-executives/)

## Implications for Jerry's Positioning

### Applying Research to Resume Strategy

#### Key Positioning Shifts

1. **Lead with business impact, not technology.** CTOs evaluate
   technology through business lens. Every technical achievement should
   connect to revenue, scale, or organizational outcome.

2. **Signal "builder" identity explicitly.** Growth-stage companies need
   people who've created, not just operated. Use "built," "established,"
   "created from scratch" language.

3. **Demonstrate influence without authority.** Staff+ positioning
   requires evidence of cross-team impact through expertise and
   persuasion, not management hierarchy.

4. **Front-load strategic thinking.** Executives spend 30 seconds on
   initial review. The first thing they see must signal organizational
   thinking.

5. **Show 12-18 month scaling capability.** Not too junior (won't add
   value) or too senior (will get bored). Right-size scope signals.

#### For Contract-to-Hire Positioning

- Explicitly address strategic contribution expectations
- Show leadership behaviors possible within trial context
- Demonstrate partnership mindset over vendor mindset
- Provide clear success criteria for conversion consideration

#### Communication Standards

- Concise, specific, quantified
- Technical-to-business translation
- Evidence over claims
- Strategic framing over task listing
